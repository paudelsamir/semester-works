## **1. Concept of Organization**

- *Organization* → A structured social unit where people work together to achieve a *common goal*.
- *Key Ingredients* [People] + [Common Goal] + [Joint Effort] = Organization
    
    *(A family, tribe, or ethnic group is **not** an organization.)*
    

**Formal Definitions**

> *Duncan*: "A collection of interacting and interdependent individuals who work toward common goals and whose relationships are determined according to a certain structure."
> 

> *Sherlekar & Sherlekar*: "A mechanism or basic framework enabling persons to work together effectively and achieve set goals through integrated group effort."
> 

---

### **1.1 Characteristics of Organization - `PACED`**

- *Human Association* → People are at the core.
- *Common Goal* → The unifying purpose (profit, service, welfare).
- *Division of Work* → Large tasks broken into specialized jobs → efficiency.
- *Hierarchy of Authority* → Chain of command; decision-making power flows from top to bottom.
- *Flexibility (Environmental Adaptation)* → Ability to adjust plans when external factors (technology, market) change.

---

### **1.2 Types of Organizations *(Based on Purpose)***

| **Type** | **Main Objective** | **Example** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Business Organization* | Profit for owners/shareholders | Surya Nepal, Buddha Air |
| *Non-profit Social Organization* | Service to clients/community | Tribhuvan University, Nepal Red Cross |
| *Cooperative Organization* | Mutual benefit of members, reduce competition | Savings & Credit Cooperatives |
| *Government Organization* | Public service, regulation, welfare | Ministries, Police, Army |
| *International Organization* | Collective welfare of member nations | SAARC, IMF, UNDP |

## **2. Organizational Goals: Concept, Purposes, and Types**

### **2.1 Concept of Goal**

- *Goal* → A desired future state the organization attempts to realize. Goals are the *reason* an organization exists; they answer *"Where do we want to go?"*

> *Etzioni*: "A desired state of affairs that organizations attempt to realize."
> 

> *Koontz & Weihrich*: "The ends towards which activity is aimed — the results to be achieved."
> 

---

### **2.2 Purposes of Goals *(Why set goals?)***

1. *Provide Guideline & Direction* → Shows where to focus all activities.
2. *Develop Good Planning* → Knowing the target helps design the action steps.
3. *Motivate Employees* → Clear expectations create willingness to perform.
4. *Optimal Resource Utilization* → Prevents waste; resources aligned with priorities.
5. *Evaluate & Control* → Goals become the *standard* against which actual performance is measured.

---

### **2.3 Types of Goals**

**A) By Level**

- *Corporate Goals* (Top Management) → Broad, organization-wide. Found in Mission & Strategy.
- *Tactical Goals* (Middle Management) → Department-level targets.
    
    *Example*: "Increase annual sales by 20%."
    
- *Operational Goals* (Lower Management) → Unit/section-level, very specific and time-bound.
    
    *Example*: "Produce 100 units of product each day."
    

**B) By Time Frame**

- *Long-term*: 5 – 10 years
- *Mid-term*: 2 – 4 years
- *Short-term*: ≤ 1 year

---

## **3. Features of Effective Organizational Goals *(Think `SMART`)***

- *Specific* → Precise and clearly defined.
    
    *Example*: "Achieve 10% profit on sales" — *not* "maximise profit."
    
- *Measurable* → Quantifiable in quality or quantity so progress can be tracked.
- *Acceptable* → Those responsible for achieving goals must agree with them → requires participation.
- *Realistic* → Achievable with available resources. Unrealistic goals cause frustration and failure.
- *Time-bound* → Has a clear deadline. Creates urgency and a yardstick for performance.

---

## **4. Goal Formulation: Approaches and Process (TD, BU, MBO)**

### **4.1 Approaches to Goal Formulation**

- *Top-down Approach* (Directive)
    
    Top executives develop goals → Disseminate to middle/lower levels → Implementation.
    
    *Use*: Fast, relies on top expertise; lower commitment risk.
    
- *Bottom-up Approach* (Participatory)
    
    Lower levels draft goals → Top management revises and approves → Final goals sent back down.
    
    *Use*: High employee commitment; time-consuming.
    
- *Management by Objectives — MBO* (Peter Drucker, 1960)
    
    Superiors and subordinates *jointly* set goals. Each individual's major area of responsibility is defined in terms of goals.
    
    *Advantage*: Creates strong ownership and motivation.
    

---

### **4.2 Goal Formation Process *(Step-by-step) (G, E, L, Brainstrorm, Consensus, A)***

1. *Assign a Group* → Include managers from *all levels* (top expertise + lower-level realism).
2. *Environmental Scanning* → Analyze *internal* (strengths/weaknesses) and *external* (political, tech, socio-cultural) factors.
3. *Determine & List Potential Goals* → Based on scanning results.
4. *Brainstorming* → Deeply discuss each potential goal's relevancy, strengths, and weaknesses.
5. *Reach Consensus* → Select the goal with majority acceptance → avoids future conflict.
6. *Determine Major Areas of Responsibility* → Assign specific tasks and authority-responsibility relationships.

---

## **5. Problems of Goal Formulation**

- *Improper Reward System* → If hitting the goal doesn't lead to reward, motivation drops.
- *Environmental Constraints* → Hard to predict future changes exactly → unrealistic, faulty goals.
- *Unwillingness of Managers* → Lack of knowledge/confidence causes reluctance to participate.
- *Resistance to Change* → Fear of job loss or new technology; employees try to maintain *status quo*.
- *Resource Constraints* → Inadequate funds, people, or materials → goals become wishful thinking.

---

## **6. Goal Succession and Goal Displacement**

### **6.1 Goal Succession *(Intentional modification)***

- *Definition* → A deliberate review and alteration of existing goals.
- *When necessary*:
    - Original goal is *already achieved*.
    - *External environment* changes significantly.
    - Existing goal proved *unachievable*.
- *Example* → A pharmaceutical company achieves its polio vaccine target → *goal succession*: shifting focus to a combined vaccine for multiple diseases.

---

### **6.2 Goal Displacement *(Unintentional shift)***

- *Definition* → Organizational energy and resources move away from original goals *without a conscious decision*.
- *Example* → A company's original goal was to be a leading *manufacturer*. Due to severe competition, it gradually pours all resources into *trading* — manufacturing is sidelined. That is goal displacement.
- *Reasons*:
    - *Goal Conflict* → Individual goals clash with organizational goals; employees pursue personal interests.
    - *Abstract Goals* → Vague goals lead to inconsistent decisions and drift.
    - *Employee Attitude* → Uncooperative staff or union pressure weakens focus on original aims.

## **7. Changing Perspectives of Organization (M,O, P, C, L)**

Views on what an organization *is* have evolved over time.

- *a) Mechanistic Viewpoint - classical theory adapts this*
    
    Organization = *Machine*.
    
    - Fixed working hours, rigid schedules, detailed job descriptions, tight control systems.
    - Ignores human creativity and imagination.
    - Works best in *stable environments* and for routine/blue-collar work (e.g., classic factory assembly line).
- *b) Organization as an Open System - non classical theory*
    
    Organization = *Living organism* interacting with its environment.
    
    - [Inputs (students, books, money)] → [Transformation (lectures, study, research)] → [Outputs (educated, disciplined graduates)].
    - All departments act as coordinated sub-systems to achieve the whole organization's objectives.
- *c) Organization as a Political System*
    - A complex arena of *coalitions* with different interests, beliefs, and values.
    - Groups constantly compete for *scarce resources* and power.
    - Understanding internal politics is key to predicting behaviour.
- *d) Organization as Culture*
    - Behaviour and decisions are shaped by shared *beliefs, rituals, and values* (basic assumptions).
    - Culture acts as a powerful, often invisible, force guiding daily actions.
- *e) Organization as a Learning System*
    - A *living, thinking* open system that continuously learns from history, experience, and the environment.
    - Adapts by developing new capabilities and sharing knowledge.
    - *Learning competency* is critical for long-term survival and strategic planning.
