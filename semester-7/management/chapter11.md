## **1. Concept of Globalization**

- *Globalization* → The integration of economies and societies worldwide through technological, economic, political, and cultural exchanges.
- Made possible by advances in *communication, transportation, and infrastructure*.
- Essentially, it is the growing international interaction of markets for *goods, services, and capital*.

*Understanding* → Globalization turns the world into a "global village." A product designed in one country can be manufactured in another using materials from a third, and sold everywhere. A manager today must think globally, not just locally.

---

## **2. Methods of Globalization**

*Organisations use various entry strategies to expand internationally.*

1. *Exporting*
    - Selling products produced in the home country to another country.
    - Simplest and most common method of entering foreign markets.
    - *Example*: A Nepali handicraft firm ships pashmina shawls to buyers in Europe.
2. *Joint Ventures*
    - A jointly owned firm created by a foreign and a domestic company, combining resources and expertise to develop new products or technologies.
    - Establishes a long‑term partnership while sharing risk.
    - *Example*: A Japanese electronics company partners with a local firm in India to manufacture and sell smartphones under a new joint brand.
3. *Franchising*
    - A company (franchisor) grants rights to another (franchisee) to use its brand name, products, and business model.
    - Franchisor provides marketing, brand, and HR support; franchisee invests local capital and operates the outlet.
    - *Example*: McDonald's, Coca‑Cola, Pepsi granting franchise rights to local entrepreneurs worldwide.
4. *Licensing*
    - A company gives another firm the right to manufacture a patented product in exchange for royalty or fees.
    - Typically used when a company owns unique technology but lacks resources to enter foreign markets directly.
    - *Example*: A pharmaceutical company licenses its patented drug formula to a foreign manufacturer to produce and sell in that region.
5. *Strategic Alliances*
    - Two firms form an alliance to gain market advantage without creating a separate legal entity or joint management of assets.
    - They can be easily dissolved.
    - *Example*: An airline alliance (e.g., Star Alliance) where member airlines coordinate schedules and share codes but remain independent companies.
6. *Subcontracting (Outsourcing)*
    - Contracting out certain production functions or processes to another firm, usually to gain cost advantages, better quality, or both.
    - *Example*: A sportswear brand designs shoes but subcontracts the actual manufacturing to a specialized factory in Vietnam.

## **3. Effects of Globalization**

### **A. Positive Effects**

- *Creation of demand for goods and services* → Global markets expand customer base.
- *Parallel growth of economy* → Trade boosts GDP, incomes, and living standards in participating nations.
- *Transfer of technology* → New technologies flow to developing countries, improving productivity.
- *Employment opportunities* → Foreign investment and export industries create jobs.
- *Recognition* → Local brands get global visibility and reputation.
- *Economic advantages* → Access to cheaper raw materials, larger talent pools, and diversified revenue.

### **B. Negative Effects**

- *Exploitation of resources* → Multinationals may over‑extract natural resources without adequate compensation or sustainability.
- *High price for necessary goods* → Sometimes, global demand drives up local prices of essential commodities.
- *Maximum advantages go to rich countries* → Wealthier nations and large corporations often capture the lion's share of benefits.
- *Threat to indigenous industries* → Small local businesses may be unable to compete with giant global players, leading to closures.

*Example*: While a global coffee chain entering a new country creates jobs and transfers barista skills, it may also drive local coffee shops out of business and pressure farmers to sell at global commodity prices.

## **4. Multinational Companies (MNCs)**

### **4.1 Meaning**

- *Multinational Company (MNC)* → A company engaged in producing and selling goods or services in *more than one country*.
- Operates worldwide through subsidiaries, affiliates, or branches.
- Typically headquartered in one country (home) and operates in several host countries.

### **4.2 Types of MNCs**

1. *Raw Material Seekers*
    - Look for the best and cheapest raw materials from local suppliers abroad, process them locally or ship them home for production.
    - *Example*: British Petroleum (oil), Anaconda Copper (mining) — extract resources globally to supply home operations.
2. *Market Seekers*
    - Enter foreign markets primarily to sell products and services; look for large or growing customer bases overseas.
    - *Example*: IBM, Unilever — set up operations in many countries to reach consumers directly.
3. *Cost Minimizers (Efficiency Seekers)*
    - Move production to countries with lower labor, material, or regulatory costs to gain competitive pricing.
    - *Example*: Nike and Apple design products in the home country but outsource or set up manufacturing in countries with lower production costs.

### **4.3 Advantages of MNCs**

- *Brings capital and investment* to host countries → stimulates economic growth.
- *Creates employment* and often provides better wages and training than local firms.
- *Transfer of technology and skills* → local workforce learns modern methods.
- *Increases competition* → forces local firms to improve quality and efficiency.
- *Wider choice for consumers* → greater variety of products at competitive prices.

### **4.4 Disadvantages of MNCs**

- *Profit repatriation* → earnings are sent back to the home country, limiting local reinvestment.
- *Exploitation of cheap labor and resources* → may pay low wages, ignore safety, or deplete natural resources.
- *Dominance of local market* → can drive small local competitors out of business.
- *Cultural erosion* → global brands may overshadow local culture and traditions.
- *Political influence* → large MNCs may unduly influence government policies in host countries to favor their interests.
