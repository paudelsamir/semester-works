## Unit 1. The Nature of Organizations _(3 Hrs.)_

- **1.1 Organization**
  **Concept:**

  A structured social unit where people work together to achieve a common goal.
  
  **Characteristics — PACED:**

  - **P**eople (Human Association): People are at the core.
  - **A**im (Common Goal): The unifying purpose.
  - **C**oordination (Division of Work): Large tasks broken into specialized jobs.
  - **E**xpertise (Hierarchy of Authority): Chain of command from top to bottom.
  - **D**ynamic (Flexibility): Ability to adjust plans when external factors change.
  
  **Types — by Purpose:**

  - **Business**: Profit (e.g., Surya Nepal, Buddha Air)
  - **Non-profit**: Service (e.g., TU, Red Cross)
  - **Cooperative**: Mutual benefit (e.g., Savings & Credit)
  - **Government**: Public service/regulation (e.g., Ministries, Police)
  - **International**: Collective welfare (e.g., SAARC, UN)
  

- **1.2 Organizational goals**
  **Concept:**

  A desired future state the organization attempts to realize. Goals answer "Where do we want to go?"
  
  **Purposes:**

  - Direction & guideline
  - Basis for planning
  - Employee motivation
  - Optimal resource use
  - Evaluation & control standard
  
  **Types:**

  **By Level:**
  - Corporate (top, broad)
  - Tactical (middle, dept-level)
  - Operational (lower, specific)
  **By Time:**
  - Long-term (5-10 yrs)
  - Mid-term (2-4 yrs)
  - Short-term (≤1 yr)
  
  **Features — SMART:**

  - **S**pecific
  - **M**easurable
  - **A**cceptable
  - **R**ealistic
  - **T**ime-bound
  
  **Goal formulation approaches:**

  - **Top-down**: Executives decide → disseminate down. Fast, less commitment.
  - **Bottom-up**: Lower levels draft → top revises. High commitment, slower.
  - **MBO** (Drucker): Superiors & subordinates jointly set goals. Strong ownership.
  
  **Goal formulation process:**

  1. Assign group (all levels)
  2. Environmental scanning
  3. List potential goals
  4. Brainstorm
  5. Reach consensus
  6. Assign responsibility
  
  **Problems of goal formulation:**

  - Improper rewards
  - Environmental constraints
  - Unwilling managers
  - Resistance to change
  - Resource constraints
  
  **Goal succession:**

  Deliberate revision of goals after achievement or environmental change.
  
  **Goal displacement:**

  Unintentional drift away from original goals due to goal conflict, abstract goals, or employee attitude.
  

- **1.3 Changing perspectives of organization**
  **Mechanistic view:**

  Organization as a machine — rigid, fixed hours, tight control. Ignores human creativity. Suits stable environments.
  
  **Open system view:**

  Organization as a living organism interacting with environment. Inputs → Transformation → Outputs + Feedback.
  
  **Political system view:**

  Arena of coalitions with different interests competing for scarce resources and power.
  
  **Cultural view:**

  Behavior shaped by shared beliefs, rituals, values — an invisible force guiding daily actions.
  
  **Learning system view:**

  Continuously learns from history and environment; adapts by developing new capabilities.

## Unit 2. Introduction to Management _(3 Hrs.)_

- **2.1 Management**
  **Definition:**

  A set of activities (planning, organizing, leading, controlling) using organizational resources to achieve goals effectively & efficiently.
  
  **Characteristics — MISSIONS:**

  - **M**ulti-disciplinary
  - **I**ntegrating
  - **S**ocial process
  - **S**ituational
  - **I**ntangible
  - **O**ngoing (continuous)
  - **N**ecessary (universal)
  - **S**ystematic/Goal-directed
  
  **Fayol's 14 Principles — DUO FACED ISSUES:**

  - **D** 1. Division of work
  - **U** 2. Unity of command
  - **O** 3. Unity of direction
  - **F** 4. Fair remuneration
  - **A** 5. Authority & responsibility
  - **C** 6. Centralization
  - **E** 7. Equity
  - **D** 8. Discipline
  - **I** 9. Initiative
  - **S** 10. Scalar chain
  - **S** 11. Stability of tenure
  - **U** 12. Subordination of individual interest
  - **E** 13. Esprit de corps
  - **S** 14. Order
  
  **Functions — POSDCoC:**

  - **P**lanning
  - **O**rganizing
  - **S**taffing
  - **D**irecting
  - **Co**ordinating
  - **C**ontrolling
  
  **Managerial hierarchy:**

  - **Top** (Board, CEO): Strategy, policies.
  - **Middle** (Dept heads): Tactical, coordination.
  - **Lower** (Supervisors): Daily operations.
  
  **Types of managers:**

  - **Functional**: Specific dept (Marketing, HR).
  - **General**: Multiple functions (Plant manager).
  
  **Managerial skills — Katz (TECH):**

  - **Technical**: Most important at lower level
  - **Human**: Equally important at all levels
  - **Conceptual**: Most important at top level
  - **Design**: Practical problem-solving at upper levels
  
  **Managerial roles — Mintzberg:**

  **Interpersonal (FaLL):** Figurehead (ceremonial), Leader (motivates), Liaison (external contacts)

  **Informational (MDS):** Monitor (gathers info), Disseminator (shares info), Spokesperson (speaks for org)

  **Decisional (NERD):** Negotiator (bargains), Entrepreneur (innovates), Resource allocator (budgets), Disturbance handler (crisis mgmt)
  

- **2.2 Emerging challenges for management**
  **Globalization:**

  World as "global village". Increased competition, managers must think globally and adapt to diverse cultures.
  
  **Technology:**

  Rapid change in IT, automation, data processing. Managers must continuously update capabilities.
  
  **Quality & Productivity:**

  Quality = satisfying customer needs. TQM involves everyone. Productivity = doing more with less.

## Unit 3. Evolution of Management Thought _(5 Hrs.)_

- **3.1 Classical**
  **Introduction:**

  Management thought evolved over time from classical (efficiency, structure) → neo-classical (human factor) → modern (systems, contingencies, quantitative).
  
  **Scientific Management — Taylor:**

  **Goal**: Improve productivity via scientific methods.
  **Principles**:
  1. Science not rule-of-thumb
  2. Scientific selection & training
  3. Cooperation
  4. Equal division of work
  
  **Administrative Management — Fayol:**

  **Focus**: Managerial functions for whole organization. 5 functions + 14 principles.
  
  **Bureaucracy — Weber (DRIFT):**

  Ideal organization based on legal-rational authority.
  **D**ivision of work, **R**ules & procedures, **I**mpersonal relationships, **F**ormal hierarchy, **T**echnical qualifications.
  
  **Limitations of Classical:**

  - Ignores human factor
  - Closed-system view
  - Oversimplified motivation (only economic rewards)
  - Rigid rules kill creativity
  

- **3.2 Neo-classical**
  **Human Relations — Mayo (Hawthorne Studies):**

  Social factors (belonging, attention, recognition) affect productivity. Informal groups exist. Hawthorne Effect: workers respond positively when managers show concern.
  
  **Behavioural Science:**

  - **Maslow**: Hierarchy of Needs (Physiological → Safety → Social → Esteem → Self-actualization)
  - **McGregor**: Theory X (lazy) vs Theory Y (self-motivated)
  - **Herzberg**: Two-factor theory
  
  **Contributions & Limitations:**

  **+**
  - Human element, informal groups, participation
  **-**
  - Overemphasis on human side, lacks complete framework for complex environments
  

- **3.3 Modern**
  **Quantitative / Management Science:**

  Mathematical models: PERT, CPM, Linear programming, MIS for decision-making.
  
  **System Theory:**

  Organization as open system of interdependent parts. Inputs → Process → Outputs → Feedback. Change in one affects all.
  
  **Contingency Theory:**

  No single best way — "It depends." Optimal style varies by situation (size, technology, environment).
  
  **Decision Theory — Simon:**

  Management is decision-making. **Bounded rationality**: Humans satisfice (choose satisfactory, not optimal) due to limited info/time.
  

- **3.4 Emerging management concepts**
  **Workforce diversity:**

  Differences in age, gender, culture, education → new ideas, innovation, better decision-making.
  
  **Outsourcing:**

  **Pros**: Cost savings, efficiency.
  **Cons**: Loss of control, data security risks.
  
  **Knowledge management:**

  Creating, capturing, sharing organizational knowledge. **Explicit** (manuals) vs **Tacit** (experience-based).
  
  **Learning organization — Senge:**

  Continuous learning culture. **5 disciplines**: Shared vision, Personal mastery, Mental models, Team learning, Systems thinking.

## Unit 5. Planning and Decision Making _(5 Hrs.)_

- **5.1 Planning**
  **Concept:**

  Primary mgmt function — deciding in advance what, when, where, why, how, who. A documented statement of goals + strategies + resources.
  
  **Types:**

  **By Hierarchy:**
  - **Strategic** (long-term, top mgmt)
  - **Tactical** (mid-term, middle mgmt)
  - **Operational** (short-term, lower mgmt)
  **Contingency**: Plan B if original fails.
  
  **Hierarchy — VAMOS:**

  **V**ision → **A**ims/Goals → **M**ission → **O**bjectives → **S**trategies.
  
  **Methods:**

  - **Top-down** (centralized, fast)
  - **Bottom-up** (decentralized, participative)
  - **MBO** (joint goal-setting)
  
  **Process:**

  1. Define mission/goals
  2. Scan environment
  3. Identify O&T (SWOT)
  4. Analyze resources
  5. Formulate plans
  6. Implement
  7. Evaluate & control
  
  **Importance — DUCCATTI:**

  - **D**irection
  - **U**ncertainty (reduces)
  - **C**ompetitive strength
  - **C**oordination
  - **A**ids resource utilization
  - **T**ime-bound (basis of control)
  - **T**eam alignment
  - **I**nnovation
  

- **5.2 Strategic planning**
  **Environmental scanning:**

  **Methods**:
  - Extrapolation (past→future)
  - Intuitive reasoning
  - Scenario building (what-if)
  - Delphi technique (expert rounds)
  
  **Identify O&T — SWOT:**

  - **S**trengths (internal+)
  - **W**eaknesses (internal-)
  - **O**pportunities (external+)
  - **T**hreats (external-)
  Best strategy = SO match.
  
  **Formulation:**

  1. Evaluate performance
  2. Review governance
  3. External analysis
  4. Internal analysis
  5. Analyze strategic factors
  6. Select option
  
  **Implementation:**

  "Working the plan" into action. Requires resource alignment, motivation, strong communication.
  
  **Quantitative tools:**

  - **PERT/CPM**: Network analysis for project scheduling, critical path.
  - **Break-even analysis**: Revenue = cost point calculation.
  - **Linear programming**: Optimizing under constraints.
  - **Gantt chart**: Bar chart for task scheduling & progress tracking.
  

- **5.3 Decision making**
  **Definition & Process:**

  Choice among alternatives.
  **Steps**:
  1. Identify problem
  2. Generate alternatives
  3. Evaluate
  4. Select
  5. Implement
  6. Evaluate
  
  **Approaches:**

  - **Classical**: Rational, full info, maximizes return (unrealistic for complex problems).
  - **Administrative (Simon)**: Bounded rationality, satisficing (first satisfactory option).
  
  **Types & Conditions:**

  **Types**:
  - Programmed (routine) vs Non-programmed (novel)
  - Operating / Strategic / Administrative
  **Conditions**:
  - Certainty (known)
  - Risk (probabilistic)
  - Uncertainty (unknown — rely on intuition)
  
  **Styles — Vroom-Yetton:**

  - **Autocratic** (decides alone)
  - **Consultative** (gets input, decides)
  - **Group** (consensus)
  

- **5.4 Problem solving**
  **Concept:**

  Deviation between actual & desired. Problem solving = minimizing deviation via information + selection.
  
  **Types:**

  **By Frequency:** Routine / Exceptional
  **By Urgency:** Urgent / Non-urgent
  **By Impact:** Overall / Partial
  **By Source:** Technical / Human
  
  **Strategies:**

  1. Separate person from problem
  2. Listen
  3. Gather info
  4. Explore alternatives
  5. Decide
  6. Create accountability
  7. Monitor
  8. Give credit
  

- **5.5 Crisis handling**
  **Phases:**

  Warning → Preparation & Prevention → Damage limitation → Recovery → Organizational learning.
  Steps: Anticipate → Contingency planning → Practice (drills).

## Unit 6. Organizing Function _(6 Hrs.)_

- **6.1 Organizing**
  **Concept:**

  Designing structure, assigning jobs, grouping tasks, delegating authority, establishing relationships for coordinated effort.
  
  **Principles — FORD USES SUBS:**

  - **F**lexibility
  - **O**bjective
  - **R**esponsibility
  - **D**elegation
  - **U**nity of command
  - **S**pan of control
  - **E**fficiency
  - **S**calar chain
  - **S**implicity
  - **U**nity of direction
  - **B**alance
  - **S**pecialization
  
  **Approaches to organizing:**

  - **Classical**: Universal principles, one best way (Taylor, Fayol, Weber).
  - **Behavioural**: Focus on people, social needs, participation.
  - **Contingency**: Structure depends on situation (technology, environment, size).
  
  **Process of structuring:**

  1. Study of jobs
  2. Grouping jobs (departmentalization)
  3. Division of labour
  4. Decide organization design (mechanistic/organic)
  5. Coordination of activities
  

- **6.2 Departmentalization**
  **Meaning:**

  Grouping jobs into manageable units based on logical arrangement.
  
  **Types — TCP FTP:**

  - **T**erritory (geographic)
  - **C**ustomer (per client type)
  - **P**roduct (per product line)
  - **F**unction (production, marketing, finance)
  - **T**ime (shifts)
  - **P**rocess (manufacturing steps)
  

- **6.3 Delegation of authority**
  **Meaning & Features:**

  Manager assigns workload + authority to subordinate.
  **Features**: Responsibility, Authority, Accountability.
  
  **Advantages:**

  - Reduces top mgmt load
  - Develops subordinates
  - Faster decisions
  - Increases motivation
  
  **Barriers:**

  - Manager reluctance (lack of trust)
  - Subordinate unwillingness (no reward)
  - Rigid organizational climate
  

- **6.4 Centralization & Decentralization**
  **Centralization:**

  Power retained at top.
  **Advantages**: Uniform policies, minimized duplication, strong coordination.
  **Disadvantages**: Overload, less development for lower mgmt, demotivation.
  
  **Decentralization:**

  Power dispersed downward.
  **Advantages**: Motivation, develops skills, quicker local response.
  **Disadvantages**: Harder uniform policies, risk of authority misuse, higher training costs.
  

- **6.5 Organic vs Mechanistic view**
  **Comparison:**

  **Mechanistic**: Rigid hierarchy, formal rules, centralised, vertical communication, stable environments.
  **Organic**: Flexible, few rules, decentralised, lateral communication, dynamic environments.
  

- **6.6 Modern organizational structures**
  **Matrix:**

  Dual reporting (functional + project manager). Efficient but can cause power struggles.
  
  **Team:**

  Self-managing cross-functional teams. Promotes creativity and innovation.
  
  **Network (Virtual):**

  Core in-house + outsourced partners. High flexibility, low overhead, less control.

## Unit 8. Motivation _(3 Hrs.)_

- **8.1 Motivation**
  **Concept:**

  Inner state that energizes & directs behavior toward goals. Answers "Why do people do what they do?"
  
  **Maslow's Hierarchy:**

  1. **Physiological** (salary, shelter)
  2. **Safety** (job security, insurance)
  3. **Social** (belonging, team)
  4. **Esteem** (recognition, title)
  5. **Self-actualization** (growth, creativity)
  A satisfied need no longer motivates.
  
  **Herzberg's Two-Factor:**

  **Hygiene factors** (policy, supervision, salary): absence causes dissatisfaction, presence doesn't motivate.
  **Motivators** (achievement, recognition, responsibility): create satisfaction & drive performance.
  Fix hygiene first, then enrich job with motivators.
  
  **Reward system:**

  - **Financial** (salary, bonus) vs **Non-financial** (praise, flexibility)
  - **Intrinsic** (job satisfaction) vs **Extrinsic** (promotion, pay)
  Should be transparent, equitable, performance-linked.
  
  **Employee participation:**

  - **QWL**: Quality of relationship between employees & work environment (fair pay, safety, growth, work-life balance).
  - **Self-managed teams**: Autonomous units that decide work allocation, schedules, methods. Fuels intrinsic motivation.

## Unit 9. Communication _(3 Hrs.)_

- **9.1 Communication**
  **Concept:**

  Exchange of information between individuals/groups. The glue of mgmt. Key: message must be received & understood as intended.
  
  **Structure:**

  **Formal org flows:**
  - **Downward** (top→bottom): instructions, policies.
  - **Upward** (bottom→top): reports, suggestions.
  - **Lateral** (peer→peer): coordination.

  **Small group networks:**
  - **Wheel**: Central hub communicates with all others (fast, centralised).
  - **Chain**: Follows formal chain of command (accurate, sequential).
  - **All-channel**: Everyone communicates freely (high participation, slow).
  - **Circle**: Neighbour-to-neighbour (limited, structured).
  - **Y**: Two arms converge through a central person.
  
  **Process:**

  Sender → Encoding → Channel → Decoding → Receiver → Feedback (Noise affects all stages).
  
  **Types:**

  - **Formal** (official channels)
  - **Informal** (grapevine)
  - **Interpersonal** (face-to-face)
  - **Non-verbal** (body language)
  
  **Barriers:**

  - **Process barrier**: Breakdown in encoding/decoding/feedback steps.
  - **Physical barrier**: Distance, noise, poor infrastructure.
  - **Psychological barrier**: Emotions, perceptual bias, ego, stress.
  - **Semantic barrier**: Jargon, ambiguous language, different interpretations.
  - **Technological barrier**: Bad network, incompatible tools, poor digital literacy.
  
  **Enhancing communication:**

  - **Improve communication**: Use simple language, active listening, confirm understanding.
  - **Improve environment**: Open culture, reduce noise, proper setting.
  - **Improve mindset**: Be empathetic, patient, non-judgmental.
  - **Improve clarity**: Structure messages, avoid jargon, be specific.
  - **Improve tool & how to use**: Choose right channel, train on tools, leverage multiple media.

- **12.4 Business culture in Nepal**

- **12.5 Major problems of businesses in Nepal**
  **Problems — CLIP DR PM:**
