## **1. Definition of Management**

- *Management* → A set of activities *(planning, organizing, leading, controlling)* aimed at organizational resources to achieve goals *effectively* and *efficiently* in a changing environment.

> *Griffin*: "Management is a set of activities… directed at an organization's resources… with the aim of achieving organizational goals effectively and efficiently."
> 

> *Sherlekar & Sherlekar*: "Management is a process involving coordination of human and material resources through the functions of planning, organizing, staffing, leading and controlling to accomplish stated objectives."
> 

Management = *What managers do* to convert resources into results.

---

## **2. Characteristics of Management (mgmt is all about accomplishing `MISSIONS`)**

- *Universal (needed everywhere)* → Needed in all types of organizations (business, hospital, school, government).
    
    [Example: A small local shop needs management just like a large multinational company.]
    
- *Goal-directed (success through objectives)* → Exists to achieve predetermined objectives. Success is measured by goal attainment.
- *Continuous Process (ongoing cycle)* → An ongoing cycle; does not stop as long as the organization exists.
    
    *Example*: Production, sales, and accounting activities run continuously → management never pauses.
    
- *Integrating Process* → Brings together *men, machine, material, and money* and coordinates them into a unified whole.
- *Intangible* → Cannot be seen, but felt through order, discipline, and results.
    
    [Visible signs: rules, output, work climate.]
    
- *Multi-disciplinary* → Draws knowledge from Economics, Psychology, Sociology, Engineering, Mathematics, etc.
- *Social Process* → Primarily about handling people, managing relationships, and satisfying human needs at work.
- *Situational* → No single "best way" to manage. Action depends on the specific situation.

---

### **2.1 Management as Science and Art**

- *As a Science*
    - Body of systematized knowledge (concepts, principles, theories).
    - Establishes cause‑effect relationships.
    - Principles are verifiable and have universal application.
    - *Limitation*: Human behavior prevents exact prediction → not a *pure* science.
- *As an Art*
    - Application of personal skill and creativity to get results.
    - Improves with practice; no one "best" way.
    - *Example*: Like painting, managing gets better the more you do it.
- *Conclusion*: Management is both a science (knowing *why*) and an art (knowing *how*).

### **2.2 Management as a Profession**

- *Profession* requires: specialized knowledge, formal education, code of conduct, service motive.
- *Management today* meets most criteria:
    - Formal management education (MBA, BBA).
    - Professional bodies (e.g., American Management Association, All India Management Association).
    - Evolving ethical codes.
    - Growing social responsibility.

## **3. Principles of Management — Henry Fayol's 14 Principles** (`DUO FACED ISSUES`)

*Use as general guidelines for managerial decision-making.*

1. *Division of Work* → Specialization increases efficiency.
    
    [Example: In a restaurant, one chef handles grilling, another salads — each becomes faster and more precise.]
    
2. *Authority and Responsibility* → Two sides of the same coin. Give authority equal to responsibility.
    
    [If a supervisor is responsible for meeting a deadline, he must have the authority to assign overtime.]
    
3. *Discipline* → Obedience, sincerity, respect for rules. Best achieved through good leadership, not just punishment.
4. *Unity of Command* → One subordinate receives orders from *only one boss*. Violation causes confusion and diluted accountability.
    
    [Example: A salesperson should report to only one sales manager, not two.]
    
5. *Unity of Direction* → One head and one plan for a group of activities having the same objective. Ensures coordinated effort.
6. *Subordination of Individual Interests to General Interests* → Organizational goals come before personal goals.
    
    *Example*: An employee must prioritise the company's project deadline over personal leisure.
    
7. *Fair Remuneration* → Pay should be just and satisfactory to both employee and employer. Reduces conflict.
8. *Centralization and Decentralization* → Balance power at the centre with delegated authority. The degree depends on the situation and capacity of subordinates.
9. *Scalar Chain* → The formal line of authority from top to bottom. Can be "short-circuited" (gang plank) in emergencies for faster communication, keeping superiors informed.
10. *Order* → "**A place for everything and everything in its place**." Also, right person in the right job.
    
    [Example: Tools arranged systematically in a workshop; the accountant placed in finance, not marketing.]
    
11. *Equity* → Kindness + justice. Treat employees fairly without discrimination (caste, gender, religion). Builds loyalty.
12. *Stability of Tenure of Personnel* → Long-term employment security. Frequent turnover harms efficiency and morale.
    
    [Example: A software firm retaining skilled developers for years → builds deep product knowledge.]
    
13. *Initiative* → Encourage employees to suggest new ideas and better methods. Boosts creativity and ownership.
14. *Esprit de Corps* (Spirit of Co‑operation) → Promote team spirit and unity. "Union is strength."
    
    *Means*: frequent communication, appreciation, and minimizing conflict.

## **4. Process and Functions of Management (P, O, S, D, Coord, Contr)**

*Management is a continuous chain of interrelated functions:*

1. *Planning (5w1h)* → Deciding in advance: what, when, how, who, where.
    - Involves goal setting, forecasting, and choosing the best course of action.
    - *Example*: A company plans to launch a new product by December — sets production timeline, budget, and marketing channels.
2. *Organising* → Arranging tasks, resources, and people into a structure.
    - Defines authority‑responsibility relationships, creates departments.
    - *Example*: After planning, the firm sets up a new product development department, assigns a project head, and allocates budget.
3. *Staffing* → Filling positions with the right people and keeping them competent.
    - Includes: recruitment, selection, training, remuneration, performance appraisal.
    - *Example*: Hiring design engineers, training them on new software.
4. *Directing* → Guiding, supervising, motivating, and leading subordinates.
    - Includes effective communication and command without resentment.
    - *Example*: A manager explains the vision, gives clear instructions, and inspires the team to meet the product launch deadline.
5. *Coordinating* → Harmonising all activities and efforts to avoid gaps or overlaps.
    - Ensures every department works like organs of one body.
    - *Example*: The production, sales, and finance departments sync their schedules so the product is manufactured, funded, and sold on time.
6. *Controlling* → Measuring actual performance against standards and correcting deviations.
    - Steps: set standards → measure → compare → take corrective action.
    - *Example*: Quality checks during production ensure the product meets safety standards; if not, process is adjusted.

## **5. Managerial Hierarchy & Types of Managers**

### **5.1 Levels of Management (T — M — L)**

- *Top-Level Management* (Board of Directors, CEO, President, General Manager)
    - Sets overall objectives, strategies, policies.
    - Exercises overall control.

- *Middle-Level Management* (Department heads: Production Manager, Sales Manager, Finance Manager)
    - Translates top‑level goals into departmental targets.
    - Coordinates and implements plans within their function.

- *Lower-Level Management* (Foremen, Supervisors)
    - Directly oversee operative workers.
    - Ensure daily work is done correctly and on time.

 Each level is subordinate to the one above.

### **5.2 Types of Managers (by Scope)**

- *Functional Managers* → Responsible for a specific department/function.
    
    [Example: Marketing Manager, Human Resource Manager.]
    
- *General Managers* → Oversee multiple functions or a whole independent unit (e.g., a branch or division).
    
    [Example: Plant Manager overseeing production, finance, and HR within a factory.]

## **6. Managerial Skills — Katz's Model (tech, human, conceptual, design skills)**

*Four essential skills, with importance varying by level:*

- *Technical Skill*  Most important at Lower Level → Proficiency in specific methods, tools, techniques.
    
    [Example: Accountant using spreadsheet software, mechanic using diagnostic equipment.]
    
- *Human Skill*  Equally important at all Levels → Ability to work with, understand, and motivate people; build cooperation.
    
    [Example: Resolving a conflict between team members, listening actively.]
    
- *Conceptual Skill*  Most important at Top Level → Seeing the "big picture", thinking strategically, understanding how the whole organisation fits together.
    
    [Example: CEO envisioning how a new technology trend might reshape the industry.]
    
- *Design Skill*  Critical at Upper Levels → Ability to find practical solutions to problems, not just spot them.
    
    [Example: Designing a new workflow to cut costs by 15% instead of just noting that costs are high.]

## **7. Managerial Roles — Mintzberg's 10 Roles**

*Grouped into three categories: Interpersonal, Informational, Decisional.*

**A. Interpersonal Roles** (arise from formal authority) - `FaLL into relations`

- *Figurehead* → Symbolic duties: attending ceremonies, signing documents.
    
    [Example: Plant manager giving awards at the annual function.]
    
- *Leader* → Motivating, training, staffing, guiding subordinates.
- *Liaison* → Building external relationships with other organizations, government, industry groups.
    
    [Example: Meeting officials of a partner firm to discuss collaboration.]
    

**B. Informational Roles** (processing information) - `MDS`

- *Monitor* → Constantly scanning environment for useful information.
    
    [Example: Reading market reports, competitor analysis.]
    
- *Disseminator* → Sharing collected information with subordinates.
- *Spokesperson* → Transmitting information to outsiders.
    
    [Example: Press conference announcing quarterly results.]
    

**C. Decisional Roles** (making tough choices like `NERD`)

- *Entrepreneur* → Initiating improvement projects, innovation.
    
    [Example: Launching a new digital service to capture online market.]
    
- *Disturbance Handler* → Taking corrective action during crises.
    
    [Example: Resolving a sudden strike, handling a product recall.]
    
- *Resource Allocator* → Distributing human, financial, material resources.
- *Negotiator* → Bargaining with suppliers, unions, dealers.
    
    [Example: Negotiating a long-term raw material contract at lower price.]

## **8. Emerging Challenges for Management (globalization, tech, QA and productivity)**

- *Globalisation*
    - World → "Global village". Free flow of goods, services, ideas across borders.
    - Increased competition (WTO, regional trade pacts) → managers must think globally, adapt to diverse cultures and regulations.
        
        [Example: A Nepali handicraft firm now competes with international online sellers; manager must adopt e‑commerce and global logistics.]
        
- *Technology*
    - Rapid change in computers, automation, data processing.
    - Alters planning, decision‑making, control, and communication.
    - Managers must continuously update technological capabilities and foresee disruptions.
        
        [Example: A bank manager must lead the shift to mobile banking and cyber‑security.]
        
- *Quality Assurance and Productivity*
    - *Quality* = ability to satisfy customer needs. Rising expectations force continuous improvement.
    - *TQM* (Total Quality Management) → involves everyone – from suppliers to customers – in improvement.
    - *Productivity* → doing more with less, eliminating material/time/effort waste.
        
        [Example: A garment factory uses lean manufacturing to reduce fabric waste and improve stitching accuracy, thereby raising both quality and productivity.]
